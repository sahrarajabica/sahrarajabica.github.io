Common questions

Before you book

Do you work with individuals and small teams, or only large companies?

Both. We provide solutions, services and consultations to individuals, small businesses and larger organizations. The scope changes with size; the method does not.

Will our people be able to keep working during a migration?

Yes. Content moves in planned waves, usually outside working hours, and each wave is verified before the old location is set to read-only. Nobody is asked to stop working while files copy.

What happens to our permissions when files move to SharePoint?

We map them before migrating. File share permissions rarely translate one-to-one, so we review who has access to what, agree on a cleaner structure with you, and then apply it in SharePoint.

We want Microsoft 365 Copilot. Where should we start?

With your data. Copilot answers using whatever each person can already open, so overshared sites and stale content show up in its answers. An AI readiness assessment finds those gaps first.

Can you build something specific to our organization?

Yes. Alongside our ready-to-use products we build custom Microsoft 365 add-ons for needs the standard tools do not cover.

Quick reference

Where should a file live?

Microsoft 365 gives you three places for files. Most confusion disappears once everyone uses the same rule.

Mine

OneDrive

Your own work: drafts, notes and files you have not shared with a team yet.

Ours

Microsoft Teams

A team's day-to-day work: files that belong with a project and its conversations.

Everyone's

SharePoint

Content for a department or the whole organization: policies, templates and records.

Guides

Start here

File shares to SharePoint: seven decisions to make first
  1. What not to move. Duplicates, abandoned folders and content past its retention date stay behind.
  2. Where each share lands. A team site, a Teams channel, or personal OneDrive.
  3. Who owns each destination. Every site needs a named owner before content arrives.
  4. How deep folders become structure. Long paths and deep nesting break sync; flatten where you can.
  5. Which permissions carry over. Decide what is rebuilt cleanly and what is mapped as-is.
  6. The order of waves. Start with a willing team, finish with the most complex share.
  7. When the old share goes read-only. Set the date and tell people before the wave starts.
SharePoint on-premises to SharePoint Online: a pre-migration checklist
  1. Inventory site collections, sizes, last-modified dates and owners.
  2. List customizations: farm solutions, custom workflows, InfoPath forms and scripts.
  3. Decide the replacement for each customization or agree to retire it.
  4. Check for checked-out files, very large files and unsupported characters.
  5. Agree on the new site architecture, including hub sites and navigation.
  6. Run a pilot migration on one representative site and review the report.
  7. Prepare communications and training for each wave.
Microsoft 365 Copilot readiness: six questions to answer
  1. Which sites are shared with everyone, and should they be?
  2. Where does sensitive content live, and is it labelled?
  3. How much content is outdated enough to mislead an AI answer?
  4. Who are the first users, and what tasks will they use it for?
  5. What are staff allowed to do with AI output, and where is that written down?
  6. How will you measure whether it is helping?

If two or more of these have no clear answer, begin with an assessment before buying licences.

Governance in plain terms: five rules that keep Microsoft 365 tidy
  1. Every site and team has at least two owners.
  2. New sites are created through one request route with a stated purpose.
  3. External sharing is on only where the business needs it.
  4. Inactive sites are reviewed on a schedule, then archived or deleted.
  5. Access is granted to groups, not to individuals one at a time.
SharePoint permissions in plain terms

Every SharePoint site starts with three groups. Put people in the right group and you rarely need anything else.

  1. Owners have full control. They manage the site and decide who gets in. Keep this group small.
  2. Members can add, edit and delete content. This is where most of the team belongs.
  3. Visitors can read but not change anything.

Give access to a group, not to a single file or person, and avoid special permissions on individual folders. Every exception is something you will have to find and review later.

The first week after a migration: a short checklist
  1. Confirm the old location is read-only, so nobody keeps working in the wrong place.
  2. Check that OneDrive sync is working on each person's computer.
  3. Replace old shortcuts and mapped drives with links to the new sites.
  4. Collect questions in one place and answer them where everyone can see.
  5. Review the sharing links created in the first days.
  6. Set the date when the old location will be switched off, and tell people.
Before you turn on external sharing: five things to decide
  1. Which sites may be shared outside the organization, and which may never be.
  2. Who is allowed to share externally: everyone, or site owners only.
  3. The default link type. Links for specific people are safer than links anyone can open.
  4. Whether links to outside people expire, and after how long.
  5. How often guest access is reviewed, and by whom.

Glossary

Terms you will hear from us

Tenant
Your organization's own Microsoft 365 environment, with its users, data and settings.
Migration wave
A batch of content moved together on a set date, then verified before the next batch.
Permission inheritance
A folder or file taking its access rules from the site above it. Breaking it creates exceptions to track.
Oversharing
Content that more people can open than need to, often through old links or broad groups.
Web part
A building block added to a SharePoint page, such as a list, a form or a custom tool.
Governance
The agreed rules for who can create, share, keep and delete content, and who checks.
Adoption
The work of helping people use the new tools in their daily tasks, beyond switching them on.
AI readiness
How prepared your data, security and people are for tools such as Microsoft 365 Copilot.
